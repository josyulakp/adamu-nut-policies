ADAM-U nut pick-and-place

ADAM-U's right arm picks a 22 mm hex nut off the table and sets it on the green pad. Two end effectors: the stock dexterous hand (scripted expert and a behaviour-cloned policy on held-out seeds) the YAM linear_4310 parallel-jaw gripper (scripted expert and a behaviour-cloned policy trained the same way), and the YAM gripper with FlexiTac tactile pads running Touch in the Wild and DexScrew policies (with the live pad readings). Task definition

Robot
ADAM-U from pnd_mujoco, base fixed, right arm (7 joints) and hand. Waist, neck and left arm held still.
Start
Nut lying flat, x 0.26–0.38 m, y −0.34 to −0.18 m, yaw uniform over one 60° hex period.
Goal
Green pad, x 0.26–0.36 m, y −0.08 to 0.02 m, randomised per episode.
Observation
26 values: arm joint angles and velocities, grip command, pinch point, nut position, sin/cos of 6×yaw, target position.
Action
8 values at 20 Hz: 7 arm joint position targets plus a grip command from 0 to 1 that drives a thumb–index–middle synergy.
Success
Nut centre within 20 mm of the pad centre, flat on the table (tilt under 15°), not touching the hand, held for 0.5 s.
Episode
At most 300 steps (15 s).
Simplified hand
Fingers close with real contacts. Once the thumb and index or middle finger both touch the nut with the grip past 0.45, the nut is welded to the hand; it is released when the grip drops below 0.35.
FlexiTac tactile variant
YAM fingertips replaced by flat 24 × 64 mm FlexiTac pads with 32 × 12 taxels at 2 mm pitch. Each taxel reads the penetration of a virtual 0.5 mm elastomer into the nut (stiffness 5000 N/m, saturating at 10 N), the same model as the FlexiTac Isaac Sim sensor.
YAM gripper variant
The right hand is replaced by the YAM linear_4310 parallel-jaw gripper from MuJoCo Menagerie, on a 35 mm adapter at the wrist-roll joint. The grip command maps to finger travel (95 mm open to 5 mm past closed, 50 N pads). The grasp is purely frictional, with no weld.
Co-training (YAM)
Image-based Diffusion Policy (head and wrist RGB plus joint angles, no nut pose) trained on randomised simulation demos mixed with a few demos from a "real robot" stand-in: a second simulation with an unseen look, 150 ms latency, softer servos, imperfect gravity compensation and lower pad friction. Replace the stand-in with recorded robot data to run it for real.
